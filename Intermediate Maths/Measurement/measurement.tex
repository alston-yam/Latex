    \section{Units}
    \begin{enumerate}
        \item Litres (L) used for liquids
        \item Metres (m) used for length
        \item Grams (g) used for mass
    \end{enumerate}

    \section{Prefixes}

    \begin{enumerate}
        \item milli
        \item centi
        \item kilo
        \item mega
    \end{enumerate}

    Prefixes are words we put in front of units (metres, litres, grams). 

    There are $1000$ milli in one unit, (E.g: $1$ gram = $1000$ milligram, $1$ metre = $1000$ millimetre).
    $100$ centi is in one unit, $1$ kilo is $1000$ units, and $1$ mega is $1000000$ units.

    We rarely ever use centi in anything other than metres.

    \section{Area}
    \begin{theorem}[Circle]
        \[\text{Area} = \pi r^2, \text{Circumference} = 2\pi r\]
    \end{theorem}

    \begin{theorem}[Triangle]
        \[\text{Area} = \frac{1}{2}bh\]
    \end{theorem}

    \begin{theorem}[Trapezium]
        \[\text{Area} = \frac{1}{2}(a+b)h\]
    \end{theorem}
    
    \section{Measurement Conversions}

    \begin{theorem}
        If we fill a $10$cm by $10$cm by $10$cm box with water, we would've used $1$ litre of water, and $1$ litre of water will weigh exactly $1$ kilogram.
    \end{theorem}

    Think about it, in a cubic metre box (1m by 1m by 1m), we can actually fit $100 \times 100 \times 100 = 1000000$ smaller boxes that are 1cm by 1cm by 1cm. 
    
    Now, think of a square. If we were to double the side length of a square, what happens to its area? What about if we triple it? Think about a cube also. What happens to its volume if we double, triple its side lengths?

    \section{Problems}
    
    \begin{problem}
        How big is a $1m^3$ box, in $mm^3$?
    \end{problem}

    \begin{problem}
        I have $8$ litres of water that I want to store in a cube box. What is the dimension of the box that I'll need?
    \end{problem}

    \begin{problem}
        How many millilitres are in a megalitre?
    \end{problem}

    \begin{problem}
        Car fuel costs $\$2.70$ per litre. Bob spent $\$108$ on fuel, and used it to travel $100$km. How many km can Bob travel with $1$ litre of fuel?
    \end{problem}

    \begin{problem}
        I have a triangle, and I will double the length of each side to get a new triangle. By what factor did the area of my triangle increase by?
    \end{problem}

    \begin{problem}
        A metal disc has a diameter of $12$cm. A hole is cut from the centre of the disc with radius one third of the original disc. What is the area of the disc remaining?
    \end{problem}

    \begin{problem}
        How much string is required to tie up a parcel $2\times5\times12$cm allowing $10\%$ extra for the knot at the top? Write your answer in metres.
    \end{problem}

    \begin{problem}
        Sam has a box that is $12$cm by 20cm by 7cm. He wants to pack his miniature cars in the box. They are in packets which measure $6$cm by 3cm by 2cm. What is the maximum number of miniature cars that hecould pack in the box?
    \end{problem}

    \begin{problem}
        A rectangular whiteboard is $4$ times as long as it is wide. If it were $7$ metres shorter and $2$ metres wider, it would be a square. What is the area of the whiteboard? Express your answer in square centimetres.
    \end{problem}

    \begin{problem}
        Tanya can shovel $8m^3$ of dirt in $3$ hours. Nikora can shovel $6m^3$ of dirt in $4$ hours. 
        
        What is the total amount of dirt that they will both have shovelled if they work at the same rate for $204$ hours over the next month?
    \end{problem}

    \begin{problem}
        A trapezium has a base that is three times longer than its parallel side. It has a height that is two-thirds the base. If the trapezium has an area of $676cm^2$, what is the length of the base?
    \end{problem}

    \begin{problem}
        Thre is a cube and a cuboid which have volumes in a ratio of $7:24$. The cuboid's length is $4$ times the length of the cube. The cuboid's width is $3$ times the length of the cube. What fraction of the cube's side is the height of the cuboid?

        Express your answer as a fraction in its simplest form.
    \end{problem}

    \begin{problem}
        A lift has a weight limit of $1500$kg. If the average weight of the people in the lift is $80$kg and the combined weight of all the people is $100$kg over the limit, how many people are in the lift?
    \end{problem}

    \begin{problem}
        A cubic lamington cake, with side $x$cm, is covered in $1$cm thick icing on \textbf{all} sides. Find an expression for the volume of icing.
    \end{problem}

    \begin{problem}
        I bend a 12cm piece of wire into a square and then into a $3, 4, 5$ triangle. What is the difference in areas of these two shapes?
    \end{problem}

    \begin{problem}
        The four interior angles of a quadrilateral are $x^{\circ}, (3x)^{\circ}, (x+20)^{\circ}, (x+40)^{\circ}$. Find the size in degrees of the largest angle in this quadrilateral.
    \end{problem}

    \begin{problem}
        $27$ small cubes are put together to make a bigger cube of side length $3$. If all sides but the bottom are painted red, how many small cubes have only two sides painted?
    \end{problem}

    \begin{problem}
        A square ended cuboidal package has a volume of $300$ cubic centimetres. If the dimensions are all in whole centimetres, what are the dimensions of the package that has the least surface area?
    \end{problem}

    \begin{problem}
        I have a length of rope that I want to use to mark off a rectangular area on the grass, I use the whole length every time. I can make many different rectangles, two I made have an area of $96$cm$^2$ and an area of $225$cm$^2$. What is the greatest rectangular area I can make with this length of rope?
    \end{problem}
